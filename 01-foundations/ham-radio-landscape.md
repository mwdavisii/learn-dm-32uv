# Ham radio landscape

A short orientation to **amateur (ham) radio** in the US: what it is, how licensing tiers work, and what you specifically can and can't do before and after your Tech exam.

## What is ham radio?

A federally-regulated service (FCC Part 97) for non-commercial two-way radio as a hobby, public service, and technical experimentation. Licensed operators get access to wide swaths of spectrum from HF (shortwave) up through microwaves, for talking locally, talking to satellites and the ISS, bouncing signals off the moon, digital modes, contests, emergency comms (ARES, SKYWARN), and general tinkering.

## The three US license classes

| Class | Exam | Privileges unlocked |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| **Technician** | 35 questions, multiple choice | All amateur privileges on 6 m, 2 m, 1.25 m, 70 cm, 33 cm, 23 cm (VHF/UHF); plus limited HF on 10 m, small CW slivers on 80/40/15 m. This is what you're studying for. |
| **General** | Another 35-question exam | Adds large HF allocations — the "talk around the world" bands. Same VHF/UHF privileges. |
| **Extra** | 50 questions, hardest | Adds small additional HF subbands + everything. Mostly a completeness goal. |

All licenses are good for **10 years** and are free to obtain (except the ~$15 FCC application fee). No Morse code required anymore for any class.

## What Tech unlocks for you specifically

The DM-32UV operates on 2 m (144–148 MHz) and 70 cm (420–450 MHz), both of which are full Technician privileges. After you pass:

- Full VHF/UHF voice privileges: analog FM repeaters, DMR repeaters (once you also have a DMR ID), simplex contacts
- APRS, packet, satellites that operate in the VHF/UHF window
- Foxhunts, nets, SKYWARN, parades and public-service events
- Technically: limited HF on 10 m, but you need an HF radio for that — not this HT.

## What an unlicensed person can legally do

- **Listen to anything.** Monitoring is unrestricted. Your radio can receive ham bands today, no license needed.
- **Talk to nobody on ham bands.** Even a brief "test" transmission is unlicensed operation.
- **Talk on GMRS, FRS, MURS, CB** where licensed (you have GMRS) or where no license is needed (FRS, MURS, CB).
- **Transmit in genuine emergencies** on any frequency (Part 97.403) — not a loophole for testing.

## Ham vs GMRS vs FRS vs MURS vs CB — quick table

| Service | License | Power | Bands | Use |
|---------|---------|-------|-------|-----|
| **Ham (amateur)** | Yes, exam | Up to 1.5 kW (varies) | HF, VHF, UHF, microwave | Hobby, wide experimentation |
| **GMRS** | Yes, $35/10 yr, no exam | Up to 50 W mobile / 5 W HT | UHF 462/467 MHz, 30 channels | Family, business-adjacent |
| **FRS** | None | 2 W max | Shares GMRS channels 1–22 | Bubble-pack family radios |
| **MURS** | None | 2 W max | VHF, 5 channels | Low-power business/personal |
| **CB** | None | 4 W / 12 W SSB | 27 MHz, 40 channels | Truckers, off-road |

Ham is the only service with exam-gated access, and also the only service with wide technical/experimentation allowances (digital modes, band edges, custom equipment).
