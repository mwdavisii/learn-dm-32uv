# Simplex Calling Frequencies — 2 m & 70 cm FM

> **Source:** ARRL Band Plan, pulled 2026-04-18 — <https://www.arrl.org/band-plan>

## Intro

[Simplex](../glossary/simplex.md) operation means transmitting and receiving on the **same frequency** (no repeater, no offset). For the user, this is **receive-only until a Technician license is earned** — see the [RX-only rule](../01-foundations/rx-only-before-license.md). A **"calling frequency"** is a band-plan convention: hams make initial contact there, then **QSY** (change frequency) to another simplex channel to continue the QSO, keeping the calling channel clear for others.

## Primary Calling Frequencies

- **2 m FM national calling frequency: `146.520 MHz`** — the most-programmed simplex frequency in North America; often the first channel hams check when mobile or portable.
- **70 cm FM national calling frequency: `446.000 MHz`** — the UHF counterpart; less busy than 146.520 but widely monitored.

## Notes

- **Range (5 W HT, simplex):** typically **1–5 miles in urban/suburban** terrain; **5–15 miles in open terrain with elevation** on either end. Line-of-sight rules dominate at VHF/UHF — an extra 20 feet of antenna height beats most gain upgrades.
- **Etiquette:** Call CQ on `146.520` or `446.000`; once you establish contact, **move (QSY) to another simplex channel** to continue the conversation. Don't camp on the calling frequency — keep it clear for other stations trying to make initial contact.
- **Codeplug placement:** These get programmed into the **Memphis 2 m** and **Memphis 70 cm** zones of the DM-32UV codeplug in **Phase 3**, with **TX disabled** until the Technician license is in hand.
- **Band plan drift:** ARRL band plan allocations shift every few years. Any entries below should be treated as `#unverified` if not re-confirmed against the live ARRL page at programming time.
