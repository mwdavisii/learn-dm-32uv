# Callsign

A unique identifier issued by the FCC to a licensed operator. Different services use different formats:

- **Amateur (ham):** 1–2 letters + a digit + 1–3 letters. Examples: `K4AM`, `W4BS`, `KD9XYZ`. Issued sequentially; vanity calls available for a fee. The digit is the call-area region at time of issue, not where you currently live.
- **GMRS:** `W` + 1 letter + 3 digits + letter, typically. Example: `WQXX123`. Covers your whole household under one call.
- **Part 90 commercial:** `K`, `N`, or `W` + 3 digits + 2 letters. Business use.

When operating, you identify with your callsign:

- **Ham:** every 10 minutes during a QSO and at the end. Format: "`KD9XYZ`" spoken phonetically if needed.
- **GMRS:** at the end of a conversation, or every 15 minutes in extended use.

You don't have a ham callsign yet (Tech exam pending). Your GMRS callsign is on your FCC license record.
