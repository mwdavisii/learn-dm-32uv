# Repeater

A fixed, usually tower-mounted radio station that **listens on one frequency and retransmits on another in real time**, extending the effective range of handheld and mobile radios that are below the horizon from each other.

A typical 2 m FM repeater sits on a 200-foot tower, listens on 146.220 MHz (input), and rebroadcasts at higher power on 146.820 MHz (output). Two HT operators miles apart can hear each other clearly through it, even when they can't work each other directly ([simplex](simplex.md)).

To use a repeater, your [channel](channel.md) must be configured with:

- Correct output frequency as RX
- Correct input frequency as TX ([duplex](duplex.md) offset)
- Correct access tone ([ctcss](ctcss.md) / [dcs](dcs.md) for analog) or [color-code](color-code.md) (for DMR)

Owners ("trustees") maintain repeaters out of pocket. Most have a code of conduct — identify properly, don't hog, pause between transmissions so others can break in. Hams speak of "the machine" and "keying up" and "kerchunking" (keying briefly to test — generally discouraged unannounced).

See: [simplex](simplex.md), [duplex](duplex.md), [channel](channel.md), [ctcss](ctcss.md), [color-code](color-code.md)
